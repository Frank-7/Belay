                  What this experiment proves—and what it leaves open
                
                

                  The client process can stop after the provider commits, then a
                  new process can recover from stored intent and provider
                  evidence. A repeat of the original operation can reuse its
                  identity. Unknown outcomes stop for review.
                

                

                  This single-machine prototype uses a synchronous, honest
                  provider with permanent keys and authoritative lookup. Real
                  providers have retention limits, pending requests, delayed
                  status, permissions, and different recovery APIs. There is no
                  fraud detection, insurance, real-model evaluation, or
                  distributed lease here.
                

              

            

          

        

        

          The product in three steps
          

            

              01 / RECORD
              
Give the action a lasting identity.

              

                Save the refund’s amount and identity before sending it. A new
                model decision must not silently replace that intent.
              

            

            

              02 / RECONCILE
              
Ask the provider what happened.

              

                Recover an existing receipt. Send the original request only when
                the provider’s contract makes that safe and permission still
                exists.
              

            

            

              03 / STOP WHEN UNKNOWN
              
Make uncertainty visible.

              

                If the provider cannot establish an outcome, keep the case
                unresolved. Do not label it successful or retry blindly.
